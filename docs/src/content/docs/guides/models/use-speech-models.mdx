inference.rs supports twelve speech-related model families:

- **Parakeet**: NVIDIA's FastConformer speech recognisers (CTC, RNN-T and TDT heads), and the cache-aware
  Nemotron streaming ASR, served via `/v1/audio/transcriptions` with word and segment timestamps.
- **Whisper**: OpenAI's encoder-decoder speech recogniser (large-v3-turbo, large-v3, tiny to large, `.en`), served via
  `/v1/audio/transcriptions` with word and segment timestamps, up to 100 languages identified or forced.
- **Nemotron-3 Diarization**: who speaks when (up to 8 speakers), served via `/v1/audio/diarization`.
- **Streaming Sortformer v2**: who speaks when (up to 4 speakers), from its `.nemo`, also via `/v1/audio/diarization`.
- **Silero VAD**: voice activity detection, served via `/v1/audio/vad`; it also cuts long recordings for Parakeet.
- **Qwen3-ASR**: an audio-in decoder that transcribes 30 languages, through `/v1/audio/transcriptions`
  (whole-clip segments, no word timestamps) or `/v1/chat/completions`.
- **Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe**: an audio-in decoder on Nemotron-H (30B-A4B) with a speaker branch, through
  `/v1/audio/transcriptions` (word and segment timestamps from its CTC alignment head) or `/v1/chat/completions`.
- **GLM-ASR**: an audio-in decoder (GLM-ASR-Nano) that transcribes up to 655 s through `/v1/audio/transcriptions`
  (whole-clip segments, no word timestamps) or answers through `/v1/chat/completions`.
- **Audio Flamingo 3**: an audio-in decoder that transcribes up to 600 s through `/v1/audio/transcriptions` and
  reasons about speech, sound and music through `/v1/chat/completions` (NVIDIA OneWay Noncommercial licence).
- **MERaLiON-2**: an audio-in decoder on Gemma 2 that transcribes up to 300 s through `/v1/audio/transcriptions`
  and answers questions about speech through `/v1/chat/completions`; see the
  [model family notes](/guides/models/model-family-notes/#meralion-2).
- **Ultravox**: an audio-in decoder on Llama 3.2 that answers questions about audio through `/v1/chat/completions`,
  from llama.cpp's GGUFs; see the [model family notes](/guides/models/model-family-notes/#ultravox).
- **Voxtral** (Mini, Small): an audio-in decoder that transcribes 8 languages through `/v1/audio/transcriptions`
  (whole-clip segments, no word timestamps) and answers questions about audio through `/v1/chat/completions`; see the
  [model family notes](/guides/models/model-family-notes/#voxtral).
- **Voxtral Realtime**: multimodal model accepting audio input for speech transcription through
  `/v1/chat/completions`.
- **Dia**: dedicated text-to-speech model served via `/v1/audio/speech`.
- **Kokoro**: an 82M-parameter text-to-speech model with preset voices, also served via `/v1/audio/speech`.

Qwen3-ASR, Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, GLM-ASR, Audio Flamingo 3, MERaLiON-2, Ultravox, Voxtral and Voxtral Realtime are classified as multimodal models because audio is an input modality. Parakeet and
Whisper are dedicated transcription models, and Dia and Kokoro are dedicated speech models.

## Parakeet: speech recognition

```bash
inference serve -m nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3
```

The auto-loader recognizes Parakeet from the transformers layout (`config.json` with `model_type`
`parakeet_tdt`, `parakeet_rnnt` or `parakeet_ctc`, `processor_config.json`, `tokenizer.json`,
`model.safetensors`), or from NVIDIA's `.nemo` (the file, a directory holding one, or a Hugging Face repo whose
only checkpoint it is). A `.nemo` also loads the models with no transformers port, such as the hybrid RNN-T/CTC
FastConformers (`nvidia/stt_en_fastconformer_hybrid_large_pc`, decoded with its RNN-T head), and the
English streaming model; Nemotron-3.5 loads from its transformers files. NVIDIA's own `q8_0.gguf` of
these models (`general.architecture = asr`) loads too, its 8-bit weights dequantized at load: transcripts track
the full-precision model's to within a few words on hard audio, where the `.nemo` matches it exactly. A repo's
transformers files win over the GGUF beside them, so pass the downloaded `.gguf` path to load it.

A finer GGUF can be written from a `.nemo` with `inference quantize asr -m <.nemo, directory or repo> -o <file>
--policy <name>`. `--policy` takes a preset or the type every matrix takes; `q8_0`, the default, reproduces
NVIDIA's file byte for byte. `q8_0-decoder` keeps
the decoder and joint at F16, about 2% larger, which removed a third to two thirds of the 8-bit word errors in
our measurements. `q6_k-mix` (Q6_K, the feed-forward at Q8_0, the decoder at F16) is about 6% smaller than
NVIDIA's file and still more accurate. `--keep <name fragment>=<type>` sets the type of any other matrices. `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` transcribes 25 European languages and identifies the language
itself. Audio in any format the decoder reads (WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG, ...) at any sample rate is resampled to
16 kHz; recordings up to 24 minutes are transcribed in one pass, the bound NeMo gives for these models' full
attention.

`/v1/audio/transcriptions` takes OpenAI's multipart form. `response_format` is `json` (the default),
`text`, `srt`, `vtt` or `verbose_json`; `verbose_json` carries the duration and segments, and words too
when `timestamp_granularities[]` includes `word`. Timestamps are on the encoder's 80 ms grid. Parakeet
accepts and ignores `language`, `prompt` and `temperature`: it identifies the language itself.

Decoding is greedy unless `beam_size` (1 to 16, an inference.rs field) asks for a beam: NeMo's batched MALSD
search for TDT heads, its default beam search for RNN-T ones, matching NeMo token for token. Without a language
model a beam gains little; on clean LibriSpeech, TDT's MALSD cut the word error rate by about a tenth and RNN-T's
beam not at all, at several times greedy's decoding time. CTC heads decode greedily only.

`diarization_model` (an inference.rs field) names a loaded diarization model to run over the same upload while
the audio is transcribed. Each word then goes to the speaker whose turn it overlaps most (the nearest turn when
it overlaps none), segments also break where the speaker changes, and segments and words carry a `speaker`
index; `srt` prefixes each cue with `Speaker N: ` and `vtt` names it in a `<v Speaker N>` voice span.

### Long recordings

Loaded with a Silero VAD (`vad_model_id` in the engine spec, or `TranscriptionModelBuilder::with_vad_model_id`),
Parakeet cuts a recording longer than 5 minutes at its silences into windows of at most 2 minutes, transcribes
each, and joins them with their timestamps in place; stretches without speech are skipped, and the 24-minute
bound no longer applies. Without a VAD, a recording over 24 minutes is cut instead at its quietest moments
(the lowest-energy 100 ms in the last 20 s before each 2-minute mark). The model manager takes the VAD as `vad`
in `/v1/manager/load`, an inventory path, and the web UI offers the folder's VAD models beside a speech
recognition model.

```python
spec = t.EngineSpec(
    model=t.ModelSelectedTranscription(model_id="nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3", vad_model_id="silero-vad-v6.gguf")
)
```

### Streaming a whole recording

Through the C ABI and its bindings, `inference_transcription_stream_open` (Python `transcription_stream`, C#
`TranscriptionStream`) transcribes a recording as a stream of events instead of one blocking call: `started` with
the windows the model cut, then a `window` (its text and words) and a `progress` as each finishes, and last `done`
(the `verbose_json` transcript), `canceled` or `error`. The request takes `"path"`, a local file decoded to 16 kHz
mono as it reads (up to 12 hours, never held whole at its own rate), in place of audio bytes, and `"from_seconds"`
to resume at a window's end: the windows are planned over the whole recording again and the finished ones skipped,
so the rest come out as in an uninterrupted run. A cancel takes effect before the next window. `"max_window_seconds"`
(5 or more) plans shorter windows at any length of audio, cut at the VAD's silences: Parakeet, like NeMo, can skip a
sentence in a long window (a 2-minute window of a telephone call lost whole sentences that 15-second ones kept), but
under 5 seconds it can switch language from one window to the next.
`inference_diarization_stream_open` (`diarization_stream`, `DiarizationStream`) does the same for diarization: a
`progress` after each of the model's chunks and the usual response in `done`, in one pass, so a speaker keeps its
number across the whole recording.

Without an engine, `inference_audio_probe` (Python `probe_audio`, C# `ProbeAudio`) reads a file's duration, rate,
channels and codec from its header, and `inference_audio_decode` (`decode_audio`, `DecodeAudio`) writes a WAV copy
of it as it decodes (resampled, downmixed to mono on request), for playback or a waveform. Both read what the
transcription calls read, Opus included (in Ogg, Matroska or WebM, behind inference-audio's default `opus` feature).

## Nemotron-3 Diarization: who speaks when

```bash
inference serve -m nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization
```

Nemotron-3 Diarization (a streaming Sortformer) tracks up to 8 speakers, giving each a speech probability for
every 10 ms of audio. It runs its offline configuration: chunks of 340 encoder frames (2.72 s) with 40 frames of
lookahead, an arrival-order speaker cache carrying who has spoken across chunks. A speaker's segments are its
runs of frames past `threshold` (0.5); `response_format=rttm` gives NIST RTTM lines for scoring tools.

## Streaming Sortformer v2: who speaks when, from a `.nemo`

```bash
inference serve -m nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2.1
```

Streaming Sortformer v2.1 tracks up to 4 speakers, giving each a speech probability for every 80 ms of audio.
It loads straight from NVIDIA's `.nemo` checkpoint (the file, a directory holding one, or a Hugging Face repo)
and runs the schedule the checkpoint ships: chunks of 188 encoder frames (15 s) with one frame of context each
side, behind a 188-frame speaker cache. Requests and responses are those of Nemotron-3 above; only
`frame_seconds` differs.

```python
spec = t.EngineSpec(model=t.ModelSelectedDiarization(model_id="nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2.1"))
```

## Nemotron streaming ASR

```bash
inference serve -m nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b
```

`nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b` (English) and `nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b` (multilingual) are
FastConformer RNN-T recognisers trained for cache-aware streaming: causal convolutions and attention limited
to a window of chunks. They load from the transformers layout like Parakeet and are transcribed over the whole
recording with the attention window they were trained with, which gives the streaming result; like Parakeet,
they take 24 minutes in one pass, and longer recordings in windows, cut at a VAD's silences or, without one, at
the audio's quietest moments. Nemotron-3.5 takes the request's `language` (`de-DE`, `de`, ... from its
`processor_config.json`) as a prompt, or identifies the language itself when none is given, and
`verbose_json` reports it either way. A language outside its list is refused.

```bash
curl http://localhost:1234/v1/audio/transcriptions -F file=@clip.wav -F language=de-DE -F response_format=verbose_json
```

### Streamed transcription

These checkpoints can also transcribe audio as it arrives. A session takes mono audio in pieces of any size, and
each push returns the text the audio so far completes. The model emits text a chunk at a time:
- the English model's chunk is 14 frames, 1.12 s;
- 3.5's is 4 frames, 0.32 s.

So a short push often adds nothing. Finishing returns the rest and the whole transcript in the session's
`response_format`. That transcript is the one the upload endpoint gives for the same audio: the model makes the
same decisions chunk by chunk as over the whole recording. Audio at another `sample_rate` is resampled to the
model's 16 kHz.

On CUDA a session runs at about 50x real time with the English model, so live audio keeps well ahead. On CPU it
runs at about real time.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Python">

```python
import inference_rs as ir

with ir.Engine(spec) as engine:
    with engine.transcription_session('{"sample_rate": 24000, "language": "en-US"}') as session:
        for pcm16 in microphone_chunks():  # little-endian 16-bit mono at 24 kHz
            print(session.push(pcm16), end="", flush=True)
        last, transcript = session.finish()
```

Over HTTP, `GET /v1/realtime?intent=transcription` serves these sessions as OpenAI's Realtime transcription
sessions. The OpenAI SDKs connect unchanged:

```python
from openai import AsyncOpenAI

client = AsyncOpenAI(base_url="http://localhost:1234/v1", api_key="unused")
async with client.realtime.connect(extra_query={"intent": "transcription"}) as conn:
    await conn.session.update(session={"type": "transcription", "audio": {"input": {
        "format": {"type": "audio/pcm", "rate": 24000}, "turn_detection": {"type": "server_vad"}}}})
    await conn.input_audio_buffer.append(audio=base64_pcm16)  # as the microphone fills
    async for event in conn:
        if event.type == "conversation.item.input_audio_transcription.delta":
            print(event.delta, end="", flush=True)
```

See [OpenAI compatibility](/reference/openai-compatibility/#realtime-transcription-sessions) for the events and the
energy-based server VAD.

Other notes:
- **Models:** sessions take a cache-aware streaming checkpoint only. A model that transcribes whole recordings (Parakeet TDT, RNN-T, CTC) refuses one. A session stays on the model it opened on.
- **Decoding:** sessions decode greedily, and audio is taken at 8 to 192 kHz.
- **Limits:** a model holds up to 64 open sessions, shared by every API key. Each holds about 15 MB of caches and is released when it finishes, when it is closed (dropped, freed or disposed), when a push fails, or after 10 minutes without a push. So a live client pushes its silence too.
- **Timing:** resampled audio reaches the model about 2 ms late, as an upload's does.
- **Uploads:** sessions share the model with uploads, so a push waits behind any whole recording the model is transcribing. Give live audio a model of its own.

## Silero VAD: voice activity

Silero VAD (v6, 16 kHz) gives a speech probability for every 32 ms of audio and cuts speech segments from
them exactly as the reference `get_speech_timestamps` does. It loads from a small GGUF (about 1.2 MB, F32)
made from the official weights, which `mlx-community/silero-vad-v6` republishes:

```bash
hf download mlx-community/silero-vad-v6 model.safetensors --local-dir silero
cargo run -p inference-models-speech --example silero_vad_gguf -- --weights silero/model.safetensors silero-vad-v6.gguf
inference serve -m silero-vad-v6.gguf
```

A GGUF file, a directory holding one, or a Hugging Face repo holding one all load. Audio at other rates is
resampled to 16 kHz, where the reference runs its separate 8 kHz model or decimates 48 kHz, so segments at
those rates can differ slightly from the reference's.

## Whisper: speech recognition

```bash
inference serve -m openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo
```

Whisper loads from the transformers layout (`config.json` with `model_type` `whisper`, `generation_config.json`,
`tokenizer.json`, `model.safetensors`); the special token ids, languages and alignment heads come from
`generation_config.json`, so the v1 to v3 sizes and the English-only `.en` checkpoints load alike. It is decoded as
OpenAI's `transcribe` does by default, with word timestamps on:

- The recording goes in 30 s windows (the last one zero-padded, as OpenAI's `pad_or_trim` does); each window is
  decoded with timestamp tokens, and the next window starts after the last word of the last complete segment.
- **Temperature fallback:** each window is decoded greedily first; when its text compresses better than 2.4 to 1
  (zlib, so it repeats itself) or its tokens average a log-probability under -1, it is decoded again at 0.2, 0.4, ...
  1.0 (sampled, from a fixed seed, so a transcript repeats). A window whose no-speech probability is over 0.6 with a
  low log-probability is skipped as silence. `temperature` above 0 decodes every window at that temperature instead.
- **Beam search:** `beam_size` (1 to 16) searches that many hypotheses per window at temperature 0, `patience` (an
  inference.rs extension, 1 by default) lets `beam_size * patience` finish before the best by log-probability over
  length is taken, as OpenAI's `BeamSearchDecoder`; fallback temperatures sample.
- **Context:** `prompt` goes before the first window as previous text (`<|startofprev|>`, at most 223 tokens, as
  OpenAI's `initial_prompt`), and each window takes the previous windows' text the same way;
  `condition_on_previous_text=false` (an inference.rs extension) turns that off, and a window decoded above 0.5 starts
  the context afresh, as OpenAI's does.
- `language` (an ISO-639-1 code such as `de`, or the name, `german`) sets the language token; without one the model
  identifies it from the first window, and `verbose_json` reports it. An English-only checkpoint takes English
  only. A language the checkpoint has no token for is refused.
- Word times come from the cross-attention of the checkpoint's alignment heads, matched to the audio with dynamic
  time warping, then held to the segment timestamps, as OpenAI's `word_timestamps` does.
- `/v1/audio/translations` writes an English translation instead (large-v3; turbo was not trained to translate).
- Not yet: loading GGUFs.

`scripts/whisper_parity.py` checks a build against OpenAI's `whisper` package on given clips, with `--decoding`
choosing OpenAI's default, plain greedy, beam 5 or a prompt. On synthetic speech in F32, base.en's transcripts and word
times match exactly (to 0.000 s, over four windows) in each mode, except where a window falls back to a sampled
temperature on both sides, whose draws differ; earlier, word times fell within 0.02 s for large-v3-turbo (FP16),
large-v3 and small.

## Qwen3-ASR: audio in, transcript out

```bash
inference serve -m Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-hf
```

Qwen3-ASR loads from the transformers `-hf` repos, from the original ones (`Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B`, weights under
`thinker.`), and from llama.cpp's GGUFs (see the [model family notes](/guides/models/model-family-notes/#qwen3-asr)).
`/v1/audio/transcriptions` decodes the model's transcription prompt greedily and returns its transcript:

```bash
curl http://localhost:1234/v1/audio/transcriptions -F file=@clip.wav -F model=default -F language=en
```

- `language` (ISO-639-1, or the model's language name) forces the transcript's language, as the reference
  processor does by starting the answer with `language <name><asr_text>`; one of its 30 languages only. Without
  it the model identifies the language, which `verbose_json` reports.
- The model gives no word timings of its own: without an aligner, `verbose_json`, `srt` and `vtt` carry the whole
  clip as one segment, `timestamp_granularities[]=word` adds no words, and `diarization_model` is refused. With
  one (below), they work as for a model that times its words.
- The encoder attends within 8 s windows; a clip takes 13 tokens a second, so long recordings fill the context.

Through `/v1/chat/completions`, send the clip as an `audio_url` part; the answer keeps the model's
`language <name><asr_text>` prefix.

### Word times from Qwen3-ForcedAligner

`Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-hf` (or the original `Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B`, whose repo has no
`tokenizer.json`: give the `-hf` one with the model's `tokenizer`) times a transcript's words on its audio. Load it
beside Qwen3-ASR and name it in `alignment_model` (an inference.rs extension):

```toml
command = "serve"

[[models]]
model_id = "Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-hf"

[[models]]
model_id = "Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-hf"
```

```bash
curl http://localhost:1234/v1/audio/transcriptions -F file=@clip.wav -F model=Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-hf \
  -F alignment_model=Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-hf -F response_format=srt
```

- The aligner reads the clip and the transcript's words (letters, numbers and apostrophes; a CJK character each),
  each followed by two `<timestamp>` slots, in one pass; its head classifies each slot's time in 80 ms steps, and
  out-of-order times are mended as Qwen's reference does. Words come back as the transcript writes them, punctuation
  included, so segments and subtitle cues break at sentences.
- It covers clips of up to 400 s (its 5,000 steps) and its 11 languages (Chinese, Cantonese, English, German,
  Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Korean, Japanese; Japanese and Korean are split at spaces, not by
  the reference's morphological tokenizers). Another language, a longer clip, or a model that does not align is the
  request's error.
- `diarization_model` then attributes the timed words to speakers.
- On OpenAI's `jfk.flac` and a 108 s reading, the word times equal transformers' `Qwen3ASRForTokenClassification`
  on the same transcript, except two boundaries in 334 that differ by one 80 ms step (BF16 here, F32 there).

```bash
inference serve -m nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B
```

The checkpoint is NVIDIA's NeMo layout (`llm.*` for the decoder, `perception.*` for the speech encoder, speaker
branch and projector). `/v1/audio/transcriptions` decodes the model's transcription prompt greedily; through
`/v1/chat/completions`, send the clip as an `audio_url` part. A clip takes 12.5 tokens a second.

With `response_format=verbose_json` and `timestamp_granularities[]=word` (or `srt` and `vtt`), the checkpoint's word
timestamp head (`ctc_timestamp.*` and `alignment_tokenizer/`) times the transcript: a CTC forced alignment over 40 ms
frames, ported from transformers' `align_word_timestamps`, whose word times ours matched exactly in our tests. The head
runs as the audio GGUF stores it: dense from a BF16 one, at Q8_0 from the Q8_0 recipe below. Segments
then break at sentence ends and pauses; without the head (an audio GGUF written before it), the transcript is one
segment. It adds a second pass of the speech encoder, about 2 s per 40 s clip on the CPU.

Set `diarization_model` to the transcription model's own name and the model attributes speakers itself: it is asked for
the model card's speaker-tagged transcript (`<spk:0>` ... before each speaker's turn), the word timestamp head aligns
each tag's words on the frames where the speaker branch matched to it is active, and segments and words carry the
speaker (the text drops the tags). Naming another loaded diarization model (Nemotron-3, Sortformer) assigns its speaker
turns to the timed words instead, as for Parakeet.

In BF16 the model needs about 63 GB. For a 24 GB GPU, write a quantized text GGUF and an audio GGUF beside it:

```bash
# the decoder as llama.cpp's `nemotron_h_moe` GGUF in BF16 (llama.cpp's converter over `llm.*`), then quantized
scripts/nemotron_transcribe_text_gguf.py <llama.cpp dir> <checkpoint dir> Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-text-BF16.gguf
inference quantize gguf -m Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-text-BF16.gguf -o text.gguf --imatrix imatrix.dat \
  --tensor-type 'ffn_down_exps\.weight$=q5_0' --tensor-type '_exps\.weight$=iq4_nl' \
  --tensor-type '(attn_[qkv]|attn_output|_shexp|ssm_in|ssm_out|output|token_embd)\.weight$=q8_0'
# the audio tensors and the word timestamp head, the checkpoint's config with its alignment tokenizer, its chat template
inference quantize nemotron-transcribe-audio -m <checkpoint dir> -o audio.gguf
# optional: the speech encoder's and the word timestamp head's matrices at Q8_0
inference quantize gguf -m audio.gguf -o audio-q8_0.gguf \
  --tensor-type 'asr_encoder\.layers\.\d+\.(ffn\.net\.\d+|attn\.w_qkv|attn\.out_proj)\.weight$=q8_0' \
  --tensor-type 'ctc_timestamp\.decoder\.(transformer\.layers\.\d+\.(ffn\.net\.\d+|attn\.w_qkv|attn\.out_proj)|decoder_layers\.0)\.weight$=q8_0'
inference serve --format gguf -m <dir> -f text.gguf --mmproj audio.gguf
```

The hidden size (2688) and the experts' width (1856) are not multiples of 256, so the experts take 32-value block
types (`iq4_nl`, `q4_0`, `q5_0`, `q8_0`). With this recipe the text GGUF is 19.3 GiB and the audio one 1.55 GiB;
on a 24 GB card, paged attention then holds about 95K tokens (two hours of audio). The Q8_0 audio GGUF is
0.88 GiB, and its matrices stay 8-bit when loaded, which raises that to about 194K tokens (on the CPU, the encoder 1.5 GB less
memory); in our measurements its transcripts tracked the BF16 one's (on synthetic clips, the same word error rate
within 0.1 points and the same KL divergence from the BF16 checkpoint), and its word times the BF16 head's but for
one word boundary in 281, two frames earlier. An imatrix from audio requests
(see [online calibration](/guides/quantization/online-calibration/) and `inference quantize imatrix`) weighs the
decoder by what audio puts through it. On a GPU the audio tower runs in the model's dtype; on the CPU it runs in f32,
as transformers keeps it (the speech encoder's quantized matrices excepted).

## Voxtral Realtime: audio in, text out

```bash
inference serve -m mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602
```

The auto-loader recognizes the Voxtral Realtime layout from `params.json`,
`consolidated.safetensors`, and `tekken.json`. The earlier `VoxtralForConditionalGeneration`
architecture is not supported.

Voxtral Realtime uses the multimodal chat request shape: audio is an input content part and the
response is its transcription. The supported checkpoint is intended for transcription; the text
part in these examples does not imply support for summarization, speaker analysis, or other audio
tasks.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="HTTP">

```bash
curl http://localhost:1234/v1/chat/completions \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "default",
    "messages": [{
      "role": "user",
      "content": [
        {"type": "audio_url", "audio_url": {"url": "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-gemma/cookbook/refs/heads/main/apps/sample-data/journal1.wav"}},
        {"type": "text", "text": "Transcribe this."}
      ]
    }]
  }'
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Python">

```python
import inference_rs as ir
from inference_rs import types as t

spec = t.EngineSpec(
    model=t.ModelSelectedMultimodalPlain(
        model_id="mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602",
        arch=t.MultimodalLoaderType.VOXTRAL,
    )
)

with ir.Engine(spec) as engine, open("clip.wav", "rb") as clip:
    response = engine.chat(
        t.ChatCompletionRequest(
            model="default",
            messages=[
                t.Message(
                    role="user",
                    content=[
                        {"type": "audio_url", "audio_url": {"url": "media://0"}},
                        {"type": "text", "text": "Transcribe this audio."},
                    ],
                )
            ],
            max_tokens=256,
            temperature=0,
        ),
        media=[ir.MediaAttachment(clip.read(), "audio/wav")],
    )
    print(response.choices[0].message.content)
```

</TabItem>


## Dia: text-to-speech

`/v1/audio/speech` matches OpenAI:

```bash
inference serve -m nari-labs/Dia-1.6B
```

Dia understands dialogue speaker tags such as `[S1]` and `[S2]`, and nonverbal parentheticals such as `(laughs)` or `(coughs)`. Use them in the `input` string when you want dialogue or expressive speech.

Its sampling is set when the model loads: a speech model spec takes `generation` with any of `max_tokens`, `cfg_scale`, `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k`, and leaves the rest at Dia's defaults (`cfg_scale` 3, `temperature` 1.3, `top_p` 0.95, `top_k` 35).

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="HTTP">

```bash
curl http://localhost:1234/v1/audio/speech \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "default",
    "input": "[S1] Hello. This is a test of the text-to-speech system.",
    "response_format": "wav"
  }' \
  --output out.wav
```

- Output: raw audio bytes.
- `response_format`: only `wav` and `pcm` are read; `mp3`/`opus`/`aac`/`flac` return a validation error.
- Dia ignores `voice`, `speed`, `phonemes`, `seed` and `instructions`; Kokoro reads all but `instructions` (below).

</TabItem>

## Kokoro: text to speech

```bash
inference serve -m hexgrad/Kokoro-82M
```

Kokoro loads from its PyTorch release: `config.json`, `kokoro-v1_0.pth` (or a `model.safetensors` with
the same tensor names), and every voice pack under `voices/` (`.pt` from the release, or raw
little-endian f32 `.bin` packs of 510 x 256 values). It speaks at 24 kHz.

It also loads [audio.cpp](https://github.com/0xShug0/audio.cpp)'s Kokoro GGUF (architecture `kokoro_tts`,
such as the multilingual `kokoro-82m-q8_0.gguf`), which carries its config and all 54 voices:

```bash
inference serve -m /path/to/kokoro-82m-q8_0.gguf
```

Pass the file, or a directory holding just that GGUF; the auto loader recognizes it. Its matrices are Q8_0 and its
convolutions BF16, so its audio differs measurably from the full-precision release, though not in kind.

Kokoro reads plain text: `input` is phonemized by
[vernacula-phonemizer](https://github.com/christopherthompson81/vernacula-phonemizer) in the language the
voice's first letter names (`a`: American English, `b`: British English, `e` Spanish, `f` French,
`h` Hindi, `i` Italian, `p` Brazilian Portuguese, `j` Japanese, `z` Mandarin), then rendered into the
phoneme alphabet Kokoro was trained on. A voice with any other prefix needs `phonemes`. The phonemizer's
data downloads from the Hugging Face repo `christopherthompson81/vernacula-phonemizer-data` file by file, a language
on its first use (the default voice's at load), into the usual Hugging Face cache, so `HF_HUB_OFFLINE` and
`HF_HUB_CACHE` apply. `VERNACULA_DATA_DIR`, pointing at a copy of the phonemizer's `data` directory, takes its place.
Without the data, requests with `phonemes` still work. Long text is split at paragraphs,
sentences and clauses so each piece fits the model's context.

Request fields Kokoro reads:

- `phonemes`: Kokoro's own phoneme string, spoken in place of `input`. Characters outside Kokoro's
  vocabulary are dropped, and a string longer than the model's 510-phoneme context is split at spaces.
- `voice`: a voice the model ships, such as `af_heart` (the default when present). Several,
  comma-separated, are averaged into one voice. An unknown voice is rejected with the list of
  available ones.
- `speed`: the speaking rate; 1.0 is the model's own, higher is faster. The model spec's
  `generation.speed` sets the default.
- `seed`: seeds the vocoder's noise, so the same request gives the same audio. Without it each
  request draws a fresh seed.
- `timestamps`: answers with JSON instead of raw audio: `audio` (the `response_format` audio, base64-encoded),
  `content_type`, `duration` in seconds, and `words`, each word of `input` with its `start` and `end` in seconds and
  its canonical `ipa`. The times come from the durations Kokoro predicts for each phoneme, scaled to the audio and
  grouped into the words the phonemizer read them from; a written form read as several words (`$3.14`) is one word
  with all its readings. With `phonemes` there are no source words, so `words` is empty; Dia answers with no words.
